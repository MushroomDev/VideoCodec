The most common causes:

Quality loss is almost always caused by encoding parameters that are set too low. Try:

  • Raise the bitrate: In "Video Settings", increase the target bitrate. A safe starting point: 8–12 Mbps for 1080p, 30–50 Mbps for 4K.
  • Switch to CRF mode: Set CRF to 18–23 (lower = higher quality). CRF 18 is essentially visually lossless.
  • Use a slower preset: Changing from "veryfast" to "medium" or "slow" significantly improves quality at the cost of longer encoding time.
  • Keep the original resolution: Do not scale the video unless you have a specific reason to.

Pro tip: CRF 18 + slow preset ≈ visually lossless. Perfect for archiving.

VideoToolbox hardware acceleration activates automatically when all of the following are true:

  1. Your device is Apple Silicon (M1/M2/M3/M4) or an Intel Mac with Quick Sync support
  2. Output codec is set to H.264 or H.265 (HEVC)
  3. "Prefer hardware acceleration" is enabled in Settings

Hardware acceleration delivers 3–5× encoding speed with much lower CPU load and heat. Note: hardware encoding typically has slightly lower compression efficiency than software codecs (x264/x265) — best when speed is the priority.

Several options:

  • In Settings, lower the "Parallel tasks" count. The default is auto (= CPU core count); try setting it to 1 or 2.
  • Enable VideoToolbox hardware acceleration to offload encoding to dedicated hardware.
  • Use a faster encoding preset (ultrafast / superfast / veryfast) to reduce per-frame complexity.

CodecFlow's task scheduler automatically balances system load. You can also use Activity Monitor to limit the app's CPU scheduling priority.

  • The source was encoded with highly efficient compression (e.g. H.265 at a low bitrate), but the output codec or bitrate setting is less efficient.
  • The source is a heavily compressed streaming video and you re-encoded it with a high bitrate setting.

To fix it:
